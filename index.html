Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems · 2017

Attention Is All You Need

Ashish Vaswani · Noam Shazeer · Niki Parmar · Jakob Uszkoreit

Abstract

The dominant sequence transduction models are based on complex recurrent or convolutional neural networks that include an encoder and a decoder. The best performing models also connect the encoder and decoder through an attention mechanism.

We propose a new simple network architecture, the Transformer, based solely on attention mechanisms, dispensing with recurrence and convolutions entirely. Experiments on two machine translation tasks show these models to be superior in quality while being more parallelizable and requiring significantly less time to train.

1. Introduction

Recurrent neural networks, long short-term memory and gated recurrent neural networks in particular, have been firmly established as state of the art approaches in sequence modeling and transduction problems such as language modeling and machine translation.

Recurrent models typically factor computation along the symbol positions of the input and output sequences. This inherently sequential nature precludes parallelization within training examples, which becomes critical at longer sequence lengths.

2. Background

The goal of reducing sequential computation also forms the foundation of the Extended Neural GPU, ByteNet and ConvS2S, all of which use convolutional neural networks as basic building blocks.

Attention mechanisms have become an integral part of compelling sequence modeling and transduction models, allowing modeling of dependencies without regard to their distance in the input or output sequences.

3. Model Architecture

Most competitive neural sequence transduction models have an encoder-decoder structure. The encoder maps an input sequence of symbol representations to a sequence of continuous representations. Given those representations, the decoder then generates an output sequence one element at a time.

The Transformer follows this overall architecture using stacked self-attention and point-wise, fully connected layers for both the encoder and decoder. Each layer contains residual connections followed by normalization, helping information flow reliably through the network.

3.1 Encoder and Decoder

The encoder is composed of a stack of identical layers. Each layer has two sub-layers: a multi-head self-attention mechanism and a simple position-wise fully connected network.

The decoder adds a third sub-layer, which performs attention over the output of the encoder stack. Masking prevents positions from attending to subsequent positions.

3.2 Scaled Attention

Attention maps a query and a set of key-value pairs to an output. The output is computed as a weighted sum of the values, with weights determined by compatibility between each query and key.

Scaling the dot products helps maintain stable gradients when the dimensionality of the keys becomes large.

5. Training and Results

The model was evaluated on machine translation benchmarks. Batches were constructed so that examples with similar sequence lengths were grouped together, improving computational efficiency. Regularization and learning-rate scheduling were used to improve generalization.

5.1 Machine Translation

The Transformer achieved higher translation quality while requiring substantially less training time. Its parallel structure made better use of modern hardware than recurrent approaches.

6. Conclusion

Attention-only sequence models are faster to train and can capture long-range dependencies effectively. Future work can extend the approach to other modalities and more structured outputs.
